# Common storyboard and revision contract

Draft to bind to the selected recording and screenshots; not an approved visual review or a rendered result.

## Narrative

A small fictitious product demonstrates one workflow: turn a messy recording into a clean edit, add three visual explanations, then revise the brand without changing the story. The voice remains the timing authority. All product data is synthetic.

## Three graphics

| Sequence | Duration | Required action | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Organize | 6 s | Screenshot overview scales from 0.94 to 1 over 0.5 s; highlight three items in sequence; hold legible copy | Start, midpoint, settled-state frames |
| Review | 6 s | Screenshot detail pans toward a decision area; two status cards enter 0.2 s apart; highlight approval | Intermediate motion and text boundaries |
| Deliver | 6 s | Show completed job list; three bars grow to fixed synthetic values; brand lockup enters and holds | Bar endpoints, readable logo and screenshot |

Both renderers receive the same source screenshots, font, copy, timing, colors and layout dimensions. Animations use deterministic time. Graphics occupy the right-hand area; the shared source stays visible on the left. Captions occupy a reserved bottom band. No graphics should cover captions.

## Branding revision

Change brand name from FrameLab to SignalCut, replace the monogram, and change accent from teal (#25b9a5) to coral (#f27d62). Keep screenshot data, speech, subtitles, cut boundaries, graphic timing, duration and output dimensions unchanged. The screenshot brand label may be revised through the same explicit token substitution in both inputs.

## Review record

For each renderer, retain the storyboard contact sheet, identified defects, correction count and exact final frame references. Distinguish shared design changes from renderer integration repairs. Do not call a non-rendered storyboard visually verified.

## Comparison limits

Matched instructions support a useful practical comparison, but the same author implementing both is not a blind model evaluation. Local synthetic narration, if selected, provides no evidence about face continuity or lip sync. Original tone-based music/effects test mixing and synchronization; they do not demonstrate music supervision or professional sound design.
